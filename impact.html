Reading the panel

The three questions

Which upstream reservoirs have the greatest impact?

The upstream reservoirs whose storage change is the largest share of the target's seasonal runoff, listed by how many hops upstream they sit. Greatest impact is named first in each hop level. Click a name to re-target it, or hover to light its route to the target.

Which upstream reservoir changed its stored water the most?

The single upstream reservoir whose stored water moved the most that season — whether it filled up or drew down.

Which upstream reservoir released the most stored water?

The upstream reservoir that lost the most stored water — the likeliest source of extra water reaching the downstream reservoir.

A release here means a drop in storage, not a measured gate opening. The dashboard shows potential effects, not confirmed releases. Impact percentages are per reservoir against the target's runoff and are not additive across the nested upstream watersheds.

The numbers on each card

million m³
How much stored water rose or fell over the season, averaged across the period.
% of the season's runoff
That storage change compared with the total runoff of the target reservoir's watershed for the season — the source-to-target impact. A bigger share means a bigger effect.
reservoirs downstream
How many reservoirs sit between this one and the downstream target.

Period, season & trend

Period — results are averaged over 2001–2005, 2006–2010, 2011–2015, or 2016–2021, so single-year noise is smoothed out.

Season — Dry, Pre-monsoon, Monsoon, or Winter.

📈 Yearly trend — opens a year-by-year chart for that reservoir, one line per season. Points above the centre line ended fuller; below, emptier.
