The two main questions

1. Which upstream reservoir had the strongest overall storage influence?

This finds the upstream reservoir with the largest seasonal change in stored water, whether its storage increased or decreased.

If a reservoir ends the season with 90 million m³ more stored water, that suggests more water remained stored upstream and may have reduced downstream flow.

2. Which upstream reservoir may have added the most stored water to downstream flow?

This looks only at upstream reservoirs whose stored water decreased during the season. The reservoir with the largest decrease is highlighted.

If a reservoir ends the season with 120 million m³ less stored water, it shows the strongest potential for previously stored water to have been added back to the downstream river system.
A storage decrease does not prove that a gate was opened. Gate-release observations are not available, so this dashboard shows potential downstream addition of stored water, not measured release volume.

Key terms

Stored water
Water held in a reservoir. A seasonal increase means it ended with more stored water; a decrease means it ended with less.
Million m³ / MCM
1 MCM = 1 million cubic meters of water. The dashboard mainly writes this as “million m³” to make the unit easier to understand.
Seasonal watershed runoff
All modeled runoff generated across the complete drainage area feeding the reservoir you selected during the chosen season.
Storage change as % of watershed runoff
Compares how much a reservoir's storage changed with the amount of runoff generated across the selected reservoir's full watershed during the same season.
Watershed-runoff coverage
Shows how much of the selected reservoir's runoff is generated inside the drainage area above a particular upstream reservoir.

Why compare storage change with watershed runoff?

A storage change by itself can be hard to interpret. The same 100 million m³ can be very large in a small basin but relatively small in a very large river system.

If a reservoir's storage changes by 90 million m³ and that equals 37% of the selected watershed's seasonal runoff, the storage change was large compared with the amount of water generated across that watershed during the season.

A larger percentage therefore means the reservoir's storage behavior was more substantial relative to the amount of water available in the downstream watershed.

How to read runoff coverage

Runoff coverage = 70% means about 70% of the runoff generated above the selected reservoir comes from the drainage area located upstream of that source reservoir.
Do not add runoff-coverage percentages from different upstream reservoirs. Their watersheds may be nested inside one another, so the percentages overlap.

Simple interpretation

Storage increased: more water remained stored in the reservoir by the end of the season, which may have reduced water moving downstream.

Storage decreased: the reservoir ended with less stored water, which may indicate that previously stored water was added back to the downstream river system.

Larger runoff percentage: the storage change was large relative to the amount of runoff generated in the selected downstream watershed.

Larger runoff coverage: a larger share of the selected reservoir's watershed runoff originates in the drainage area above that upstream reservoir.
